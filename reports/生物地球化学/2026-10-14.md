> Abstract Coastal wetlands are increasingly impacted by tropical cyclones and storm surges. Seawalls and levees built to reduce these hazards disconnect wetlands from adjacent uplands, yet their hydroecological effects remain poorly understood. Using continuous hydrological observations in Chongming Island, Changjiang Estuary, China, we compared seaward and landward wetlands separated by a seawall. CTD sensor data, water quality measurements, and plot surveys revealed that seawall construction weakens tidal connectivity and reduces hydrological exchange landward.
